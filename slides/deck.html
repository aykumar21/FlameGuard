The problem

1 g is not the worst published case.

NASA-STD-6001 Test 1

An upward burn at Earth gravity. A material fails if it burns more than 15 cm. Programs use this pass.

Olson & Ferkul, Table I

Nomex, Ultem, and Mylar reach a lower limiting oxygen at lunar or Martian gravity than at 1 g.

The empty cell

Saffire-VI flew a large fire near 34% oxygen and 8.2 psia only in microgravity. That atmosphere at lunar or Martian gravity and vehicle scale has not been flown.

The solution

One cabin. Four readings.

01

Mission

Summarizes the cabin against the published limiting oxygen for the witness on the desk.

02

Atlas

Ranks the investigations for that cabin: gravity, atmosphere, and the hazard that is loudest.

03

Ember

Answers a question only from cited rows. No outside language model, so it cannot invent a flight number.

04

Brief

Prints the same state: limit, detection, suppression, spread, the open cell, and the method.

Screening math is labeled. Measured numbers stay cited.

Microgravity flights

  • Saffire I–VI and BASS for spread and scale.

What it does not claim

A comparison. Not a certification.

Not a fire probability

The exposure index compares cabins. It does not say how likely a fire is, and it does not replace NASA-STD-6001.

Not the Test 1 burn

Drop-tower limits used about five seconds. Test 1 fails a material that burns more than 15 cm. The criteria are different.

No invented pressure correction

When the cabin pressure differs from the witness test, mole fraction is compared directly and the offset is stated.

No invented limits

Catalog materials without a published row — SIBAL, cotton, PMMA, silicone, beta cloth — are not given a limiting oxygen.
